# The Engineer's Case for Medium Load

*An engineer's concerns over **medium** use or load — from Architecture to Man — and the cause, or college, those concerns serve.*

---

## 1. What "medium load" means to an engineer

Medium load is the tier where work stops being a single thought and becomes a *process*. In the `mearvk/Nintendo` studio, `medium-load` sits deliberately between `light-load` — one title, a handful of edits, the whole job held in the mind at once — and `heavy-load`, the large multi-title campaign. The medium tier is a batch: several titles sharing character tables, a set of edit scripts applied in sequence, a working set too large to hold entirely in the head but too small to need the full machinery of a campaign. To an engineer, "medium" is not a size so much as a *transition*: it is the moment the work must be organized rather than merely remembered. This document makes the engineer's case for treating that transition as the most consequential one in the whole load hierarchy.

The reason the transition matters is that medium load is where most real engineering actually lives, and where most real failures are born. The single edit rarely goes catastrophically wrong; the thousand-edit campaign is approached with such ceremony that it is usually watched closely. It is the middle — the batch of several titles, applied over a few sessions, "big enough to be useful, small enough to feel safe" — where discipline silently decays. A shared table drifts out of sync with one script. A manifest stops being updated halfway through. An offset map in `notes/` describes a layout that two titles ago ceased to be true. The engineer's case for the medium tier is a case against this quiet decay, and for the structures that hold a process together once it exceeds the span of memory.

## 2. Architecture: shared state is the defining problem

Architecturally, everything that distinguishes the medium tier from the light tier reduces to one thing: **shared state**. Under light load, a `.tbl` character map serves exactly one title, and if it is wrong, exactly one title is wrong. Under medium load, that same table is reused across several titles, and its correctness is now a property of the *batch*, not of any single edit. The architecture of `medium-load` — `manifest.tsv` indexing the batch by filename, `tables/` holding the shared maps, `scripts/` typically one per title, `notes/` carrying the cross-title offset work — is an architecture organized entirely around the safe management of state that more than one unit of work depends upon.

This is why the medium tier keeps the same text-only, no-ROM discipline as the light tier but adds an obligation the light tier does not have: *coherence across files*. A shared table is only an asset if every script that references it agrees with it. A manifest is only an index if it names every title the batch actually touches. The medium tier's architecture does not merely store artifacts; it stores artifacts that must remain consistent with one another, and the engineer's primary architectural concern here is keeping that web of dependencies true. The folder is small, but it is no longer simple, and the engineer must treat it as a system rather than a drawer.

## 3. Architecture: the loop runs many times, so the loop must be trustworthy

The dump → script → apply → verify loop does not change shape between tiers, but under medium load it changes *frequency*. The loop that ran once for a light edit now runs several times, once per title in the batch, often across more than one working session. Architecturally this means the loop can no longer be trusted because the engineer watched it; it must be trusted because it is reproducible. Each apply still writes to a separate output path, leaving originals untouched. Each verify still checks expected bytes, bounds, and the slot invariant that keeps every rename inside its original fixed-width slot. But the engineer's confidence must now come from the fact that the *same* verified loop ran identically for every title, not from having personally inspected each one.

The slot invariant earns its keep most clearly here. Because every edit stays within its original slot, a batch of several titles can be applied without any one of them altering the structural layout of a ROM. The invariant means the batch does not accumulate structural risk as it grows: ten safe, slot-bounded edits are exactly as structurally safe as one. This is the architectural property that makes the medium tier *possible* — without it, a batch would compound risk with every title, and the only safe batch size would be one. The engineer's case is that the medium tier is viable precisely because the architecture refuses to let scale erode the per-edit guarantee.

## 4. From Architecture to Man: the engineer under process

The move from architecture to the human is sharpest at the medium tier, because this is where the engineer's own limits become the binding constraint. A batch of several titles exceeds the span of working memory; the engineer can no longer hold the whole job in mind, and so the architecture must hold the parts the mind has dropped. The manifest remembers which titles are in play when the engineer cannot. The shared table enforces consistency the engineer would otherwise have to re-verify by hand. The per-title scripts externalize intent so that a session resumed on Thursday faithfully continues the work begun on Monday. Medium load is where tooling stops assisting the engineer and starts *compensating* for them — and that is a feature, not a failure.

This is the humane core of the medium tier: it accepts that a competent engineer, doing careful work, will still forget. It does not shame forgetting; it designs around it. The engineer's dignity is preserved by a system that does not require perfect recall across sessions, that makes resumption safe, that turns "where was I?" into a question answered by reading the manifest and the notes rather than by anxious reconstruction. "From Architecture to Man" here means recognizing that the medium tier's structures exist to carry the cognitive load that a human cannot, and that building them well is an act of respect for the person who will inherit the work — including the engineer's own future, forgetful self.

## 5. The engineer's concerns, enumerated

Under medium load the engineer's concerns inherit everything from the light tier — correctness, provenance, reversibility, legibility — and add three that only appear once work becomes a process. The first is *consistency*: do all the scripts agree with the shared table, and does the manifest still describe the batch as it actually is? The second is *resumability*: if the engineer stops mid-batch and returns days later, is the state recoverable from text alone, with no undocumented knowledge required? The third is *isolation*: does each title's edit remain independent, so that a mistake in one script cannot silently corrupt the output of another?

These three concerns are the signature of the medium tier, and attending to them is what separates a batch from a pile. A pile is several edits that happen to live in the same folder; a batch is several edits bound by a consistent, resumable, isolated process. The engineer's discipline at this tier is the discipline of keeping that binding true: updating the manifest the moment a title enters or leaves, regenerating or re-verifying the shared table whenever it changes, and keeping each script answerable for exactly one title so that failures stay local. The medium load does not forgive as freely as the light load, because its mistakes hide in the relationships between files rather than in any single file.

## 6. The Cause: medium load as the proof that discipline scales

The cause this document serves — trustworthy, reproducible, legally clean, evidence-first tooling — is *tested* at the light tier but *proven* at the medium one. Anyone can be careful once. The claim the studio actually makes is that care survives repetition, that the same verified loop produces the same auditable result the tenth time as the first, and that a reviewer can reconstruct a multi-title batch from text alone. Medium load is where that claim stops being an aspiration and becomes a demonstration. If the discipline holds across a batch, it is a real discipline; if it only held for the single edit, it was merely a mood.

Serving the cause at the medium tier means defending reproducibility against the specific pressures of batch work. The pressure to apply one more script without updating the manifest. The pressure to tweak the shared table for one title without checking the others. The pressure to carry some crucial offset "in your head" rather than in the notes, because you will surely remember. Each of these is a small surrender that, compounded across a batch, dissolves the auditability the whole studio depends on. The engineer who refuses these surrenders at the medium tier is the one who proves the cause can bear weight — which is the only kind of cause worth having.

## 7. The College: medium load as the engineer's maturation

If the light tier is where an engineer is admitted to the practice, the medium tier is where they are *formed* by it. This is the college in its deeper sense: not the admission but the education, the place where a practitioner learns to manage what they cannot personally hold, to trust a process over their own recall, and to design for their future self's forgetfulness. The lessons of the medium tier — externalize intent, keep shared state coherent, make work resumable, keep failures isolated — are the lessons that turn a careful technician into an engineer, because they are lessons about systems rather than about individual acts.

A college is also where standards are transmitted by example at a scale large enough to be instructive. A mentor reviewing a medium-load batch can show not just whether an edit was correct but whether the *process* was sound: was the manifest kept honest, was the shared table disciplined, could a stranger resume this batch from the text alone? These are questions the light tier is too small to pose and the heavy tier too daunting to pose gently. The medium tier is the right classroom for them — large enough to be real, small enough to be forgiving — and an engineer who has been taught well here is ready for the campaign scale of the heavy tier without being endangered by it.

## 8. The engineer's verdict on medium load

The engineer's verdict is that medium load is the *decisive* tier. It is where work becomes process, where shared state becomes the defining architectural problem, where tooling begins to compensate for human limits rather than merely assist, and where the studio's cause of reproducible, auditable research is proven rather than merely professed. It is also the tier where decay is quietest and therefore most dangerous — the middle ground that is too big to simply remember and too familiar to approach with ceremony. To respect the medium load is to respect the moment at which discipline must become structural, because memory alone will no longer hold.

The instruction that follows is correspondingly specific. Keep the manifest honest the instant the batch changes. Keep the shared tables coherent with every script that depends on them. Keep each script answerable for exactly one title, so failures stay local. Keep the whole batch resumable from text alone, assuming nothing about what you will remember. Run the full verified loop for every title, every time, and let your confidence come from its reproducibility rather than from your attention.
